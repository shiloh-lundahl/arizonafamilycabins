If your group's cabin week lands on a Saturday morning in summer, the Show Low Main Street Farmers' Market & Art Walk is worth building your day around. It's a weekly outdoor market near the Show Low Library, around 9th Street and Huning, with local produce, meats, eggs, honey, baked goods, and Arizona arts and crafts vendors — plus an art walk running alongside it. It's small-town and unhurried, which fits the pace of a mountain vacation just fine.

It's also the kind of outing that doesn't demand much planning. There's no ticket, no reservation, no itinerary to build around it beyond knowing it's a Saturday morning thing. You show up, walk around, buy what looks good, and move on with your day. For a group that's already spent the week hiking, fishing, or just decompressing at the cabin, that low-key rhythm is exactly the point.

## When it runs

This one matters up front: the market is a summer-only event. It runs Saturdays from 9am to 1pm, roughly mid-May through mid-September. If you're booking a winter or spring trip to the cabins, don't plan around it — it simply isn't happening those months. But if your group is up in the White Mountains for a June, July, August, or early-September stay, and your trip includes a Saturday morning, it's an easy one to work in before the day heats up.

## What you'll find there

Expect a mix of regional produce, meats, eggs, and honey alongside baked goods — the kind of stuff that's genuinely useful if you're cooking for a big group back at the cabin. Alongside the food vendors, there are Arizona arts and crafts booths, and the market doubles as an art walk, so there's browsing to do even if nobody in your crew is in the market for a jar of honey. It's a good one to wander through slowly with coffee in hand rather than rush through with a list.

The exact vendor lineup shifts from week to week since it's an outdoor market, so what's available in June may look a little different from what's out in September. That's part of the appeal — it's worth stopping by more than once if your trip is longer than a few days, since you genuinely won't see the same setup twice.

## Why it pairs well with a big cabin week

When you've got a house full of people — think [Parkway Lodge](/parkway-lodge/) sleeping 27 or [Mohave Cabin with Treehouse](/mohave-cabin-treehouse/) sleeping 33 — cooking becomes a group event almost by default. Someone's on breakfast duty, someone's planning the big reunion dinner, and everyone eventually asks "what's fresh nearby?" A Saturday morning stop at the market is a natural answer. Grab produce, eggs, and baked goods for the group, then treat it as part of a broader grocery and supply run into Show Low before heading back up to the cabin. It's a nice contrast to a grocery store aisle — you get to actually talk to the people who grew or made what you're buying.

Both cabins have full kitchens built for cooking at scale, so a market haul doesn't have to sit in a fridge unused. A few dozen fresh eggs and some local honey can turn a big group breakfast into something a little more memorable than the usual pancake mix, and fresh produce is an easy way to round out whatever's already on the grill that night. If your trip is built around a family reunion or a big multi-generational gathering, having something local on the table for the main dinner is a nice touch that doesn't take much extra effort.

## Making a morning of it

Since the market runs 9am to 1pm, it plays well as the first stop on a Show Low morning rather than the only stop. Wander the market, pick up what you need for the next meal or two, then swing through the rest of downtown while you're in the area. If you want more ideas for filling out the day, our guide to [things to do in Show Low](/blog/things-to-do-show-low/) covers other options nearby, and if anyone in your group wants to browse beyond the market booths, [downtown Pinetop-Lakeside shopping](/blog/pinetop-lakeside-downtown-shopping/) is a short drive away and worth combining with the same outing.

Because it wraps up by early afternoon, there's no conflict with an afternoon at Rainbow Lake, a hike, or just heading back to the cabin to start cooking with what you bought.

## A few practical notes

Go earlier rather than later in the 9am–1pm window if you want the best selection — like most farmers markets, popular items can sell out before closing time. Bring cash as a backup even if some vendors take cards, and bring a bag or two for hauling your finds back to the car. Parking and exact vendor lineup can shift week to week, so if you're planning around something specific, it's worth checking Show Low Main Street's site closer to your trip dates for the current schedule.

No. It's a seasonal market that runs Saturdays, 9am to 1pm, roughly mid-May through mid-September. It's not running during winter or spring trips, so plan accordingly if your visit falls outside that window.

**What can I actually buy there?**
Regional produce, meats, eggs, honey, and baked goods, plus Arizona arts and crafts vendors as part of the accompanying art walk. It's a solid stop for stocking up on fresh ingredients for a group meal back at the cabin.

**Where exactly is it located?**
Near the Show Low Library, around 9th Street and Huning in Show Low. It's an easy add-on to a grocery or supply run into town during your stay.
